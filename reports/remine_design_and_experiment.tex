\section{The problem}

We mine factors once, from a direction someone types in, and then trade them
forever. That doesn't hold up.

Everything here is from the window we trust --- fit 2005--2016, scored 2017--2025,
LightGBM combiner, top-50 book, full costs --- and every return is excess over the
cap-weighted CSI300 total-return index, so CRR means cumulative excess return.

Rank IC by year runs 0.1104, 0.0898, 0.0587, 0.0543, 0.0504, 0.0524, 0.0160,
0.0288, 0.0469. It falls with distance from the fit and then flattens out around
0.045. The 2023 hole is real --- IC goes negative for the smaller zoo that year.

The returns are beta, not alpha. Annual excess return correlates $-0.73$ with the
benchmark's own return and $+0.11$ with our rank IC. Every year we made money was
a year the index fell. In 2017 we had the best signal this system has ever produced
out of sample, rank IC 0.1104, and lost 5.45\% because the index rose 24.25\%.

\section{What already works: refitting the combiner}

Refit the combiner every year on data before the year being traded. Same factors
throughout, nothing re-mined, so any gain comes from the refit alone.

All five years improve, on both rank IC and return. Mean rank IC rises 35\%. This
is the only configuration out of the nine we have tested with a positive CRR, and
we don't currently do it --- production fits once and never refits again.

2023 is worth a look. A refit more than halves the loss and lifts rank IC by 86\%.
About half of what reads as a regime break is just a stale combiner.

Refreshing the \emph{factors} is worth roughly twice as much again: fresh factors
score 0.1001 and 0.1136 against 0.0526 for stale factors with a fresh combiner.
Putting both together, $\mathrm{IR}=\mathrm{TC}\cdot\mathrm{IC}\cdot\sqrt{N}$ goes
0.30 (both stale) $\to$ 0.36 (refit only) $\to$ 0.68 (re-mined and refit), which is
the first number above the usual 0.5 bar. There is a caveat on that figure in
\S\ref{sec:leak}.

\section{The design}

Two layers. A deterministic one that always runs, and an agent that can only make
it act sooner.

\textbf{Deterministic.} Each factor's rolling 63-day IC is compared to its
validation IC at admission. Healthy at 70\% of baseline or better. Soft decay below
70\% for 30 days straight, which halves its weight. Hard decay below 50\% for 60
days, which drops it from scoring but keeps the UUID and re-tests it quarterly.
Monitor daily, refit monthly, re-mine at least annually and sooner if a third of
the book hard-decays. Annual is the floor, not a safe choice: the published
half-life for this kind of factor is now around 18 months.

\textbf{The agent.} The deterministic rule can't fire until a breach has held for
30 days, so there's a month or two of lag to claw back. The agent runs weekly and
on every tier change. It can pull a refit, a re-mine, or a demotion forward. The
only thing it can delay is a soft demotion, and only with a computed market
condition attached, expiring after a quarter, capped at a quarter of the book, and
never against hard decay. If it breaks or returns nonsense, nothing happens --- the
deterministic layer carries on.

\textbf{Soft decay under a tree model.} Halving a feature's weight does nothing to
a gradient-boosted tree, and LightGBM gives us no per-factor weights anyway. The
combiner already fits one model per seed and averages them, so a soft-decayed
factor goes into half the seed models instead of all of them. Same effect, no new
machinery.

\textbf{The library.} Keep everything, forever, admitted or rejected, under its
UUID. What actually trades is a capped subset chosen at each refit: drop the
hard-decayed, drop the broken, then enforce an orthogonality gate so no two bets
are the same, then cap and replace. The orthogonality gate exists in the code and
is switched off by default.

\textbf{Directions.} Each re-mine writes its own, from statistics computed up to
that date: dispersion, breadth, the size-decile spread, volatility, return
autocorrelation, turnover, overnight gaps, industry dispersion, correlation. No
news. The 2023 rotation \emph{is} rising dispersion and an inverted size spread,
and the agent sees those as numbers with no label on them. We never tell it the
story.

\textbf{Parents.} Today selection is pure exploitation over a pool that gets thrown
away at the end of each run, and it has produced operator monoculture twice. The
library becomes an archive binned by operator, horizon, regime behaviour and decay
tier, with parents drawn 50\% from the best performers, 30\% from thinly populated
bins, and 20\% from dead factors whose regime looks like the current one.

\section{How we test it}

Replay history. At each trigger point everything uses data up to that date only,
the next year gets scored, and we never look at it again. Returns stitch into one
series per arm.

A, B, G and H are a 2$\times$2 over directions and scheduling. So $A-D$ is
re-mining, $B-A$ is the direction agent, $G-A$ is the decay agent, and
$(H-G)-(B-A)$ tells us whether the two agents help each other or get in each
other's way. C and D are already measured. E and F need no mining.

Cost is 22--26 mines. G reuses A's mine whenever their trigger dates line up, so
only the divergences cost anything extra. We measure what one mine costs before
launching any of this, and the fallback is three trigger points instead of nine.

Headline metric is stitched CRR. Alongside it, per year and per arm: ARR, IR, MDD,
rank IC, IC, ICIR, TC, turnover, cost, book size, admissions, tier counts. The
benchmark's own return sits next to every arm, because excess return here is driven
by beta and an arm that wins in a falling market hasn't necessarily found anything.

\subsection{Leakage}
\label{sec:leak}

The model's weights contain the future and no prompt takes that back. We bound what
it can do --- only the training window is disclosed, a validator strips dates and
tickers, and a direction names a mechanism rather than an answer --- and then we
measure two things.

Late-sample leakage is already ruled out. If hindsight were being used, the factors
would hold up later in the sample. Instead rank IC falls steadily and bottoms out
in 2023, the most heavily documented regime break we have and exactly where the
model would know the most. Admission also selects on past data, so a factor that
only works in the future never gets in.

The first year after a fit is not ruled out, and it matters more. Among factors
that work before the cutoff, hindsight can still pick the ones it knows last
longest. That inflates year one and fades as those picks decay, which looks exactly
like what we see. Arms A and B can't detect it either, since both write factors
with the same model and the effect cancels. And the whole case for re-mining rests
on a first-year number.

Hence arms E and F, which have no LLM in them at all. Compare year-one IC against
steady-state IC as a ratio. If ours is much higher than theirs, that's the leak.
Until then we quote the steady-state figure, around 0.045--0.05, as what to expect,
not the first-year 0.11.

\section{What this settles, and what it doesn't}

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

On clean out-of-sample data the library sits inside the normal range for a usable
equity signal on every predictive-power metric, and its first year is above it. The
system finds alpha. That was the research question and the answer is yes.

Where it goes wrong is construction. Take
$\mathrm{IR}=\mathrm{TC}\cdot\mathrm{IC}\cdot\sqrt{N}$: with IC 0.0425,
$\sqrt{300}=17.3$ and TC 0.37, gross IR comes out around 0.3. Each term on its own
is ordinary. A TC of 0.37 is normal for a long-only book, which usually runs
0.3--0.6, so there is no hidden loss to go and recover. The product is too small
because of the mandate --- 300 names, 50 holdings, long only, measured against a
cap-weighted index we have no way to hedge.

Five things say the same:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item A book holding no mined factors shows the same beta problem, $-0.64$ against
our $-0.73$. It's the construction, not the factors.
\item Raising the position cap from 3\% to 10\% left TC at 0.36. The cap isn't what
binds.
\item Going dollar-neutral lifted TC to 0.51, which confirms long-only is what
binds, and still lost money.
\item Three of our nine configurations do worse than holding no factors.
\item Our best signal year lost 5.45\%.
\end{enumerate}

The objective we optimise is a function of IC. Nothing in the system optimises IR
or CRR. The design in this document fixes the research half properly --- it watches
its own decay, re-mines against it, refits continuously --- and on the numbers
above that should take gross IR from 0.30 to somewhere near 0.68.

It won't fix a transfer coefficient that is already normal, or a benchmark we can't
track long-only, or a construction stage with no search, no admission rule and no
multiple-testing control. Turning predictive power into profit needs a second
system built for construction the way this one was built for factors, searching
over constraints, hedges, weights and rebalancing, with realised net IR as the
objective instead of IC.

If we're wrong, the replay will say so: an arm that lifts IC should then lift CRR.
Our measured correlation between annual excess return and rank IC is $+0.11$, so we
don't expect it to.

Not covered here: shorting and borrow costs, live news, real capital, and any
change to construction itself. NAV is simulated and compounds from 100M CNY, where
the impact term is small enough not to matter yet.
